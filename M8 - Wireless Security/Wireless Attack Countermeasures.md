### General Best Practices:
1. **Change Defaults:** Always change default settings on devices to enhance security.
   - **Router Password:** Set a strong, unique password.
   - **Enable Firewalls:** Activate firewalls to protect your network.
   - **Disable Remote Router Login:** Prevent external access to your router settings.
2. **SSID Management:**
   - **Cloaking:** Use SSID cloaking to make your network less visible.
   - **Non-descriptive SSID:** Avoid using recognizable names (e.g., "XYZ Corp"). Instead, use generic names like "Wi-Fi5" or "StayAway".
3. **Strong Passphrases:**
   - **Wi-Fi Password:** Use long, complex passphrases for Wi-Fi networks to make it harder to crack if hashes are captured.
4. **Firewall Packet Filtering:**
   - Place a packet filter between the access point (AP) and your organizational network to prevent direct access.

### Authentication Best Practices:
1. **Use Enterprise Authentication:**
   - Prefer **802.1X** for robust authentication.
   - **WPA3:** If possible, use WPA3 for improved security.
2. **Disable Unused Networks:**
   - Turn off Wi-Fi if it's not in use, especially in office environments with wired connections.
3. **Secure Wi-Fi Location:**
   - Place routers in secure locations to minimize unauthorized access.

### Bluetooth Security Best Practices:
1. **PIN Security:**
   - Use non-regular, unpredictable PINs to enhance security.
2. **Non-Discoverable Mode:**
   - Keep Bluetooth devices non-discoverable when not in use.
3. **Avoid Unknown Connections:**
   - Do not accept pairing requests from unknown devices.
4. **Use Encryption:**
   - Ensure Bluetooth communications are encrypted.
5. **Regularly Check Bluetooth Connections:**
   - Periodically review paired devices on your Bluetooth settings (e.g., on Android devices).
6. **Disable Bluetooth When Not Needed:**
   - Turn off Bluetooth if not actively using it to avoid potential attacks.
