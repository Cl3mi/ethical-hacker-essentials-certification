# Detailed Summary of Physical Security Controls

## Introduction to Physical Security Controls
- **Physical security controls**: Measures designed to prevent, detect, and respond to unauthorized access or incidents affecting physical resources.
- **Types of Controls**: Preventative, Detective, Deterrent, Recovery, and Compensating.

## Types of Physical Security Controls

### 1. Preventative Controls
- **Purpose**: To stop security violations before they occur.
- **Example**: Door locks prevent unauthorized access by making it difficult to enter locked areas.
- **Scenario**: Locked doors discourage exploration and unauthorized entry.

### 2. Detective Controls
- **Purpose**: To identify and record attempts to breach security.
- **Examples**:
  - Motion detectors
  - Alarm systems
  - Video cameras
- **Scenario**: Video surveillance can reveal unauthorized access attempts.

### 3. Deterrent Controls
- **Purpose**: To discourage potential intruders from attempting access.
- **Examples**:
  - Warning signs (e.g., "No non-employees allowed")
- **Scenario**: Visible signs can deter individuals from proceeding, but determined intruders might ignore them.

### 4. Recovery Controls
- **Purpose**: To restore operations and systems to a normal state after a security incident.
- **Examples**:
  - Business continuity plans
  - Disaster recovery plans
  - Backup systems
- **Scenario**: Following a disaster (e.g., flood), backup systems enable recovery of data and restoration of operations.

### 5. Compensating Controls
- **Purpose**: To provide alternative measures when primary controls fail.
- **Examples**:
  - Uninterruptible power supplies (UPS) for power outages
  - Hot sites for disaster recovery
- **Scenario**: A UPS prevents downtime during short power interruptions; a hot site allows business operations to continue if the primary site is compromised.

## Location-Based Considerations

### 1. Visibility of Assets
- **Purpose**: To manage the visibility of valuable items to prevent theft.
- **Example**: Window covers to hide valuable assets from view.
- **Scenario**: Visible Christmas presents in a car can attract thieves.

### 2. Neighboring Buildings
- **Purpose**: To consider the safety and security of the surrounding area.
- **Considerations**:
  - Crime rates in neighboring areas
  - Type of businesses nearby
- **Scenario**: A manufacturing firm should be located in a low-crime area with similar types of businesses.

### 3. Impact of Catastrophic Events
- **Purpose**: To assess and prepare for potential natural disasters.
- **Examples**:
  - Earthquakes in California
  - Hurricanes in Florida
- **Scenario**: Businesses should plan for events like earthquakes or hurricanes based on their location.

### 4. Physical Risks in Joint Tenancy
- **Purpose**: To evaluate risks associated with sharing space with other tenants.
- **Considerations**:
  - Building codes and leasing documents
  - Types of tenants and their impact
- **Scenario**: The presence of certain businesses or tenants can influence the risk profile of a shared building.

## Site Architecture Considerations

### 1. Separate Locations for Critical Infrastructure
- **Purpose**: To protect critical assets from unauthorized access.
- **Example**: Secure server rooms should be distinct from general storage areas.
- **Scenario**: Servers should be housed in secured areas, not accessible from open office spaces.

### 2. Safety Measures for Systems
- **Purpose**: To ensure the safe operation of critical systems.
- **Example**: Proper escape plans in data centers with halon fire suppression systems.
- **Scenario**: An escape plan is crucial if halon systems are used, as they deplete oxygen.

### 3. Emergency Exits
- **Purpose**: To ensure safe egress during emergencies.
- **Considerations**:
  - Properly marked and accessible exits
  - Regular drills and familiarization with exit routes
- **Scenario**: Emergency exit plans should be clear and practiced to ensure safe evacuation.

### 4. Environmental Hazard Management
- **Purpose**: To handle and mitigate environmental risks.
- **Examples**:
  - Safe storage and handling of cleaning chemicals
  - Proper sanitation systems
- **Scenario**: Ensure that chemicals are stored safely and procedures are in place for environmental hazards.

### 5. Fire Fighting Systems
- **Purpose**: To prevent and manage fire incidents.
- **Components**:
  - Fire detection (smoke, heat, and flame detectors)
  - Fire suppression (extinguishers, sprinklers)
  - Passive fire protection (fire-resistant materials)
- **Scenario**: Regular inspection and maintenance of fire systems are crucial for safety.

### 6. Physical Barriers
- **Purpose**: To restrict unauthorized access to facilities.
- **Examples**:
  - Fences
  - Bollards
- **Scenario**: Physical barriers like fences and bollards prevent unauthorized entry and protect the premises.

### Bollards
- **Purpose**: Bollards are designed to prevent vehicles from accessing certain areas, such as building lobbies.
- **Function**: They act as physical barriers to stop vehicles from crashing into sensitive areas.
- **Types**: 
  - Hydraulic bollards that can stop and lift vehicles.
  - Military-grade bollards used in bases to stop cars effectively.

### Turnstiles
- **Usage**: Common in subways, airports, and other areas to control pedestrian access.
- **Function**: Allow one person to pass at a time, often requiring ID cards or other forms of identification.

### Physical Barriers
- **Types**:
  - **Doors, Windows, and Grills**: Used to prevent unauthorized access, especially in high-crime areas.
  - **Glass and Transparent Aluminum**: Alternative materials for barriers.
- **Consideration**: Physical barriers should be included in site plans to enhance security.

### Security Personnel
- **Roles**:
  - **Trained Security Guards**: Responsible for implementing security plans and monitoring activities.
  - **Qualifications**: Can be internal or external, armed or unarmed.
  - **Front Desk Personnel**: Often replaced by security staff to manage access control and identity verification.
- **Responsibilities**: Implement security policies, handle emergencies, and manage patrolling procedures.

### First Aid and Medical Assistance
- **Importance**: Essential for responding to medical emergencies.
- **Management**: Regularly check and stock First Aid kits to ensure availability of supplies.

### Emergency Handling
- **Scenarios**:
  - **Car Accidents**: Develop plans for dealing with such incidents and instruct employees on appropriate actions.
  - **Trespassing and Crowd Management**: Have protocols for managing unauthorized access and handling large crowds or unrest.

### Physical Locks
- **Types**:
  - **Mechanical Locks**: Use springs, tumblers, and levers.
  - **Digital Locks**: Require PINs or combinations.
  - **Electromagnetic Locks**: Operate with solenoids or motors.
- **Consideration**: Update lock types according to the building's layout and security needs.

### Concealed Weapon and Contraband Detection
- **Purpose**: Detect weapons and contraband, particularly in areas where such items are prohibited.
- **Types**: X-ray systems, metal detectors.
- **Applicability**: More common in high-security environments or federal agencies.

### Mantrap
- **Function**: Securely manage access between different sections of a facility.
- **Operation**: Only one door opens at a time, with access controlled by ID, passwords, or biometrics.

### Warning Signs
- **Purpose**: Inform and deter people from accessing restricted areas.
- **Effectiveness**: Signs reduce unauthorized exploration and improve security awareness.

### Alarm Systems
- **Function**: Alert to suspicious activity or breaches.
- **Types**:
  - **Manual Alarms**: Triggered by individuals.
  - **Automated Alarms**: Include heat detectors and motion sensors.
- **Features**: Should be audible and have intervals for repeated alerts.

### Video Surveillance
- **Purpose**: Monitor and record activities for security and evidence.
- **Features**:
  - **Motion Detection**: Activates recording when motion is detected.
  - **Strategic Placement**: Cameras should cover areas like parking lots, reception areas, and sensitive zones.
- **Types**:
  - **Bullet Cameras**: Often used for their precision.
  - **Dome Cameras**: Can have motorized focus adjustment.

### Lighting
- **Importance**: Deters criminal activity by reducing dark areas where intruders can hide.
- **Types**:
  - **Continuous Lighting**: Maintains constant illumination.
  - **Movable Lighting**: Used temporarily during repairs or emergencies.

### Power Supplies
- **Types**:
  - **Standby UPS**: Common for personal computers, protects against power surges and outages.
  - **Line Interactive UPS**: Suitable for small businesses and departmental servers.
  - **Double-Conversion Online UPS**: Used for environments needing complete electrical isolation.
  - **Delta Conversion Online UPS**: Provides isolation and direct connectivity.
- **Purpose**: Ensure uninterrupted power supply to critical systems and equipment.

### Conclusion
Physical security controls encompass various methods and devices designed to protect buildings and sensitive areas. Proper implementation and regular maintenance of these controls are essential to ensuring the safety and security of an organization’s premises.
