### Introduction to Physical Security Attack Vectors
- **Overview**: Physical security threats can be categorized into natural and man-made attack vectors. Both types can impact information security similarly by destroying or compromising data.

### Natural Environmental Threats
- **Types of Natural Threats**:
  - **Floods**: Can destroy physical infrastructure and data.
  - **Fires**: Cause damage to information and businesses.
  - **Earthquakes**: Can result in significant physical damage, as illustrated by a personal anecdote from the '80s.
  - **Lightning and Thunder**: Potentially damaging to infrastructure and data.
  - **Temperature and Humidity**: Can affect the physical integrity of hardware and data storage.

- **Examples and Personal Anecdotes**:
  - **Earthquake Experience**: A story from the '80s in California highlights the danger of earthquakes and the importance of following safety procedures to avoid injury.

### Man-Made Threats
- **Types of Man-Made Threats**:
  - **Vandalism**: Riots and civil unrest can lead to significant damage to businesses and data.
  - **Theft**: Individuals or groups stealing equipment or data.
  - **Terrorism**: Targeting businesses and organizations worldwide.
  - **Social Engineering**: Manipulating individuals to gain unauthorized access or information, considered one of the most significant threats.

### Key Points
- **Impact on Data**: Whether data loss is due to natural disasters or man-made attacks, the result is often the same—compromised or destroyed data.
- **Comprehensive Security Policy**: Organizations must include both natural and man-made threats in their physical security policies to safeguard their assets effectively.
- **Major Threats**:
  - **Environmental Threats**: Natural disasters affecting infrastructure.
  - **Man-Made Threats**: Includes vandalism, theft, terrorism, and social engineering.

### Conclusion
Physical security policies must address both natural and man-made threats to ensure comprehensive protection of information and infrastructure. Understanding and preparing for these attack vectors is crucial for maintaining data integrity and operational continuity.
