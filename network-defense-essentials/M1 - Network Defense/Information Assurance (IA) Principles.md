- **Information Assurance Principles**:
  - **Confidentiality**:
    - Ensures that unauthorized individuals do not access or disclose information.
    - Achieved through encryption to prevent man-in-the-middle attacks and unauthorized data access.

  - **Integrity**:
    - Ensures that data remains unchanged during transmission.
    - Uses digital certificates and hashes to verify that data has not been altered.
    - Important for maintaining the accuracy of any data, regardless of its size.

  - **Availability**:
    - Ensures that authorized users can access information when needed.
    - Independent of other principles; focuses on making sure that information is accessible to those who are authorized.
    - Example: Multi-factor authentication should not hinder access to authorized users.

  - **Non-repudiation**:
    - Ensures that a party in communication cannot deny having sent a message.
    - Example scenario: An employee sends a threatening email but tries to deny it; non-repudiation ensures that the message can be traced back to the sender through their account.

  - **Authentication**:
    - Confirms the identity of users or entities accessing a system.
    - Answers the question "Who are you?" by verifying credentials.
    - Different from open authentication, which may allow unauthorized access; more sophisticated methods are used for secure systems.
