### Key Terminology

- **GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications)**
  - Universal system for mobile networks globally.

- **Bandwidth**
  - The throughput of information through a communication medium.

- **Access Point (AP)**
  - Device connecting wireless networks to wired networks.

- **BSSID (Basic Service Set Identifier)**
  - MAC address of an AP within a BSS (Basic Service Set), often connected to a switch network.

- **ISM (Industrial, Scientific, and Medical) Band**
  - Frequency bands used for various applications including Wi-Fi.

### Wireless Network Concepts

- **Hotspot**
  - Local Wi-Fi network made available for public or personal use, often using a cell phone to create a Wi-Fi network.

- **Wi-Fi Supplicant**
  - Device (e.g., phone, laptop) connecting to an access point.

- **SSID (Service Set Identifier)**
  - 32-bit alphanumeric designation for a WLAN.

### Wireless Encoding Methods

- **OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing)**
  - Encoding signal on multiple carrier frequencies.
  
- **MIMO-OFDM (Multiple Input Multiple Output OFDM)**
  - Combines OFDM with MIMO technology for enhanced performance.

- **DSSS (Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum)**
  - Spreads signal by multiplying it with pseudo-random noise.

- **FHSS (Frequency Hopping Spread Spectrum)**
  - Rapidly switches carrier frequencies to reduce interference.

### IEEE 802.11 Standards

- **802.11a, b, d, e, g, ac, ax**
  - Various standards for Wi-Fi networks, each with specific channel ranges, modulations, and indoor/outdoor ranges.

### Wireless Network Technologies

- **Wi-Fi**
  - Utilizes radio waves for wireless communication.
  
- **Bluetooth (802.15.1)**
  - Short-distance communication between small devices, such as cell phones and headsets.

- **RFID (Radio-Frequency Identification)**
  - Uses radio frequencies for identification and tracking, found in credit cards and other items.

- **WiMAX (802.16)**
  - Long-distance wireless networking for city-wide coverage.

### Wired vs. Wireless Networks

- **Bandwidth**
  - Wired networks generally offer higher and more consistent bandwidth compared to wireless.

- **Error Rates**
  - Wired networks have low error rates due to better error checking; wireless networks have higher error rates.

- **Security**
  - Wired networks are more secure as data leakage requires physical access. Wireless networks are more vulnerable but can use encryption.

- **Equipment Dependence**
  - Wired networks are less dependent on equipment compatibility compared to wireless networks, which may require specific hardware.

- **Symmetric vs. Asymmetric Connectivity**
  - Wired networks typically offer symmetric connectivity (equal upload and download speeds). Wireless networks often have asymmetric connectivity.

- **Resource Utilization**
  - Wired networks can support high-resource machines with ample RAM and storage. Wireless networks are suited for low-resource devices like smartphones and IoT sensors.

- **Latency**
  - Wired networks have low latency, while wireless networks experience higher latency.

### Additional IEEE Protocols

- **802.15.1 (Bluetooth)**
  - Short-range, low-speed communication.

- **802.15.4 (Zigbee)**
  - Designed for low-power IoT devices with bandwidth of 868 or 900 Mbps.

- **802.16 (WiMAX)**
  - Metropolitan area network protocol for broader city-wide connectivity.
