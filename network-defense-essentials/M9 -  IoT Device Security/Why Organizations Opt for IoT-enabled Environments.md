### Introduction to IoT

- **What is IoT?**
  - **Historical Perspective**: In the past, devices like humidity recorders in data centers were standalone and not networked.
  - **Modern IoT Devices**: Today, devices such as humidity or temperature sensors can connect via WiFi or wired methods. They send data to the Cloud and can be monitored or controlled via mobile applications.

### Application Areas of IoT

- **Industrial and Manufacturing**
  - **Examples**: Humidity and temperature sensors, manufacturing tools, robotic arms.
  - **Applications**: Automated systems for HVAC control and other industrial processes.

- **Transportation**
  - **Examples**: Automated vehicles.
  - **Applications**: Vehicles that communicate data and adapt based on real-time information.

- **Healthcare**
  - **Examples**: Heart rate monitors, pacemakers.
  - **Applications**: Devices that monitor and upload health data to the Cloud for remote review.

- **Wearable Devices**
  - **Examples**: Smartwatches.
  - **Applications**: Health monitoring and data tracking.

- **Home Devices**
  - **Examples**: Baby monitors.
  - **Applications**: Audio, video monitoring, and potentially advanced features like stress level detection.

### Reasons for IoT Adoption

- **Enhanced Connectivity**
  - **Three-Dimensional Connectivity**: Connectivity among PCs, humans, and devices ("things").
  - **Examples**: Future possibilities include networked furniture and carpets that monitor wear and tear.

### Future Trends in IoT

- **Inanimate Objects**
  - **Concept**: Objects that were previously non-networked, like carpets or desks, might become IoT-enabled.
  - **Potential Uses**: Monitoring and managing object conditions, automating maintenance tasks.

- **Mobile and Environmental Interaction**
  - **Examples**: Using tablets, phones, and laptops to interact with IoT-enabled environments both indoors and outdoors.
