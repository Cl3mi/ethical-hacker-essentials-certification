1. **Accountability and Data Ownership**: Concerns about who owns and controls data stored in the cloud, especially in public cloud scenarios.

2. **User Identity Federation**: Enables users to access multiple services with a single identity, such as logging into various services with Google or Facebook accounts. This helps manage multiple identities across different cloud services.

3. **Regulatory Compliance**: Ensuring that cloud services meet regulatory requirements applicable to your organization, such as those for financial services or healthcare. The lack of transparency from cloud providers can complicate compliance.

4. **Business Continuity and Resiliency**: The need for effective business continuity plans and working with cloud providers to ensure resilience and recovery from major disruptions.

5. **User Privacy and Secondary Uses of Data**: Risks related to unintentional data exposure or misuse, such as default sharing settings in social media platforms.

6. **Service and Data Integration**: Risks include unsecured data transmission and poor logical segregation between tenants in shared cloud environments.

7. **Incident Analysis and Forensic Support**: Challenges in forensic analysis and incident response due to distributed cloud storage and potential incompatibility with existing forensic tools.

8. **Infrastructure Security**: Issues arising from misconfigurations or mistakes by cloud service providers, such as accidental reboots of servers affecting all customers.

9. **Non-Production Environment Exposure**: Risks associated with unauthorized access to test environments, which should be protected from external exposure.

10. **Data Loss or Breach**: Potential for losing data or having data compromised due to misconfigurations or vulnerabilities in cloud services.

11. **Abuse and Nefarious Use**: Misuse of cloud resources, such as cryptocurrency mining or unauthorized storage of data.

12. **Insecure Interfaces and APIs**: Vulnerabilities in cloud APIs that could allow unauthorized access.

13. **Insufficient Due Diligence**: Cloud providers not adequately addressing security and compliance issues.

14. **Shared Technology Issues**: Risks from sharing cloud infrastructure with other tenants, which can lead to widespread impacts.

15. **Unknown Risks**: Potential unforeseen risks associated with cloud technologies and providers.

16. **Unsynchronized System Clock**: Problems arising from time discrepancies between cloud and on-premise systems affecting services like Active Directory.

17. **Inadequate Planning and Design**: Challenges in aligning cloud environment hardening procedures with on-premise practices.

18. **Loss of Operational Security Logs**: Risks related to the automatic truncation of logs, leading to loss of critical security information.

19. **Malicious Insiders**: Risks from insiders abusing cloud services or conducting illegal activities.

20. **Loss of Business Reputation**: Damage to reputation due to association with high-profile misuse of cloud services.

21. **Privilege Escalation**: Risks of unauthorized users gaining elevated access privileges.

22. **Natural Disasters**: Impact of natural events on cloud services and data availability.

23. **Hardware Failure**: Risks from failures in cloud provider hardware affecting service continuity.

24. **Supply Chain Failure**: Risks due to disruptions in the cloud provider’s supply chain.

25. **Traffic Management Issues**: Problems with monitoring and managing network traffic in the cloud.

26. **Isolation Failure**: Risks from inadequate isolation between different tenants’ data and services.

27. **Acquisition Risks**: Issues arising from a cloud provider being acquired by another company.

28. **Management Interface Compromise**: Risks related to the compromise of management interfaces for cloud services.

29. **Network Management Failure**: Failures in network management either by the cloud provider or the organization.

30. **Authentication Attacks**: Risks from weak authentication setups being exploited.

31. **VM Level Attacks**: Attacks targeting virtual machines within the cloud.

32. **Lock-In**: Challenges associated with being dependent on a single cloud provider and difficulties in migrating to another provider.

33. **Licensing Issues**: Problems related to software licensing in the cloud environment.

34. **Loss of Governance**: Risks related to loss of control over data governance, especially if data is moved to jurisdictions with different regulations.

35. **Loss of Encryption Keys**: Risks from losing encryption keys, affecting data security.

36. **Change of Jurisdiction**: Complications arising when data stored in the cloud is subject to different legal jurisdictions.

37. **Malicious Probes**: Risks from scanning or probing activities potentially compromising security.

38. **Theft of Computer Equipment**: Risks from physical theft of hardware containing sensitive data.

39. **Service Termination or Failure**: Risks from cloud services being discontinued or terminated, affecting data and service continuity.

40. **Improper Data Handling**: Risks from improper disposal or handling of data by the cloud provider.

41. **Backup Data Issues**: Problems with loss or modification of backup data.

42. **Compliance Risks**: Risks from failing to meet compliance requirements due to inadequate research or planning.

43. **Economic Denial of Sustainability**: Issues arising from the cost of cloud services being unsustainable.

44. **Security Architecture Issues**: Potential problems with the design and implementation of cloud security.

45. **Account Hijack**: Risks of unauthorized access to cloud accounts.
