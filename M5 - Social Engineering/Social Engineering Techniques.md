Here are the summarized bullet points:

**Categories of Social Engineering:**
- Human-based
- Computer-based
- Mobile-based

**Human-based Social Engineering Techniques:**
- **Impersonation**: Pretending to be a legitimate person (e.g., wearing uniforms like UPS or FedEx).
- **Reverse Social Engineering**: Setting up as an expert to get targets to seek them out and divulge information.
- **Piggybacking**: Giving someone permission to enter a secure area (e.g., holding the door open).
- **Tailgating**: Unauthorized person following someone into a secure area without their knowledge.
- **Eavesdropping and Shoulder Surfing**: Listening in on conversations or looking over someone's shoulder to get information.
- **Dumpster Diving**: Searching through trash to find useful information.

**Computer-based Social Engineering Techniques:**
- **Phishing**: Sending fake messages to trick people into providing personal information.
- **Pop-up Windows**: Fake windows that ask for login information.
- **Hoax Letters**: False warnings or fake offers.
- **Chat and Spam Messages**: Links or messages designed to steal information.
- **Scareware**: Fake alerts to scare users into taking action.
- **Phishing Variations**:
  - **Spear Phishing**: Targeting specific individuals.
  - **Whaling**: Targeting high-profile individuals like CEOs.
  - **Pharming**: Redirecting traffic to malicious sites.
  - **Spimming**: Phishing through instant messaging.

**Mobile-based Social Engineering Techniques:**
- **Malicious Apps**: Apps that look legitimate but contain malware.
- **Repackaging Apps**: Legitimate apps that are repackaged to include malware.
- **Fake Security Apps**: Apps that claim to offer security but either do nothing or are harmful.
- **SMiShing**: Phishing via SMS, asking users to click links or call numbers.

**General Tips:**
- Be cautious of unsolicited messages and emails.
- Verify the identity of people requesting sensitive information.
- Use legitimate antivirus software on all devices, including mobile phones.
- Avoid clicking on links or calling numbers from unknown sources.
