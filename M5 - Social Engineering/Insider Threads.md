Here are the summarized bullet points:

**Insider Threats and Identity Theft:**

**Insider Threats:**
- **Definition**: When trusted employees or third parties violate rules, causing information loss or exposure.
- **Causes**:
  - **Disgruntled Employees**: Upset or fired employees seeking revenge.
  - **Accident-prone Employees**: Employees who frequently make mistakes.
  - **Undertrained Staff**: Employees lacking proper training.
  - **Third Parties**: Consultants or business partners with access.

**Examples of Insider Attacks:**
- **Disgruntled Employee Example**:
  - Fred, a disgruntled employee, sends secret data to competitors using steganographic tools.
  - Motivated by money or revenge.
- **Fired Employee Example**:
  - An overworked employee discovers her job is advertised and, feeling betrayed, deletes critical company data.

**Types of Insider Threats:**
- **Malicious Insider**:
  - Deliberate harmful actions, often from disgruntled or fired employees.
  - May leave backdoors or communicate with current employees to complete attacks.
- **Negligent Insider**:
  - Unintentional harm due to mistakes or lack of knowledge.
  - Examples include accidentally deleting data or failing to secure equipment.
- **Professional Insider**:
  - Uses technical skills to exploit vulnerabilities and sell information.
  - Motivated by financial gain.
- **Compromised Insider**:
  - Financially vulnerable employees coerced into providing information.
  - Exploited by attackers offering financial incentives.

**Effectiveness of Insider Attacks:**
- **Easy to Launch**: Insiders have access and familiarity with systems.
- **Difficult to Prevent**: Insiders can cover their tracks and mimic regular activities.
- **Long-term Damage**: Attacks can remain undetected for years, leading to costly remediation.

**Conclusion**: Insider attacks are dangerous due to the access and knowledge insiders possess, making them challenging to detect and prevent.
