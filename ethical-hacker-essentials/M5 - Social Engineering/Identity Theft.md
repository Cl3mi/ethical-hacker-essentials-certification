
- **Definition**: Identity theft occurs when a criminal steals your PII (Personally Identifiable Information) such as your full name, Social Security number, national identity number, credit card number, driver's license number, or account numbers.
- **Uses of Stolen Identity**:
  - Impersonate target employees
  - Gain physical access to facilities
  - Open new accounts, including phone, wireless, and utility accounts
  - Use your financial information to pay for their expenses
  - Open banking accounts with bogus checks
  - Clone debit cards for withdrawals
- **Personal Experience Example**:
  - Small fraudulent transactions noticed and stopped by the bank
  - Attempted large purchase flagged by the bank

- **Types of Identity Theft**:
  - **Child Identity Theft**:
    - Identity stolen when the child is young, affecting their credit history when they become adults
  - **Criminal Identity Theft**:
    - Criminals use stolen financial, driver's license, insurance, and medical information
  - **Tax Identity Theft**:
    - Stolen identity used for fraudulent tax purposes
  - **Synthetic Identity Theft**:
    - Combines real and fake information to create a new identity
  - **Social Security Identity Theft**

- **Protection Measures**:
  - Safeguard PII and avoid sharing it unnecessarily
  - Use credit monitoring apps and services to track and alert on any suspicious use of personal information across the internet and dark web.
