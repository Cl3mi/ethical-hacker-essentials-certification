**Countermeasures to Social Engineering:**

- **Password Policies:**
  - Change passwords periodically.
  - Use long passphrases.
  - Avoid guessable passwords.
  - Implement account blocking after failed attempts.
  - Increase password length and complexity.
  - Keep passwords secret and do not write them down.

- **Physical Security Policies:**
  - Employees should have ID cards and uniforms.
  - Use visitor escorts.
  - Restrict access with signage and locked doors.
  - Shred unused documents.
  - Employ security personnel (receptionists, security guards).
  
- **Social Engineering Defense Strategies:**
  - Conduct social engineering campaigns to test employees.
  - Use email, text, vishing, and phishing tests.
  - Analyze campaign results and perform gap analysis.
  - Remediate by drilling failed areas and role-playing scenarios.
  - Implement separation and rotation of duties.
  - Apply the principle of least privilege.
  - Log and audit everything.
  - Monitor employees with video cameras and user behavior analytics.
  
- **Identity Theft Countermeasures:**
  - Shred sensitive documents at home and work.
  - Avoid giving personal information over the phone.
  - Secure email by quickly emptying the inbox.
  - Recognize phishing emails by checking for generic greetings, urgency, poor grammar, spoofed websites, and too-good-to-be-true offers.
  - Hover over links to check their actual destination.
  - Use anti-phishing toolbars like Netcraft or Phish Tank.
  
- **Tools for Social Engineering Prevention:**
  - Utilize social engineering toolkits (e.g., Social Engineering Toolkit, Gophish, King Phisher) to set up fake campaigns.
  - Use these tools to craft realistic phishing messages and test employee responses.
  - Provide immediate training for employees who fall for the tests.
  
- **General Tips:**
  - Continuously educate employees on recognizing and handling social engineering threats.
  - Regularly conduct and review social engineering drills to ensure employees are prepared.
  - Maintain vigilance in personal and professional online and offline activities to reduce vulnerability to social engineering attacks.
