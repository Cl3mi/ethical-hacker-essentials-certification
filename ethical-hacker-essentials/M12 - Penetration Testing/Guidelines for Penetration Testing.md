
1. **Establish Parameters**:
   - Define objectives, limitations, and justification for procedures before starting a pen test.
   - Ensure that any conflicts or changes are communicated and documented with the client.
   - Example: Pen testers got arrested during a physical test due to jurisdiction issues, highlighting the need for clear, documented agreements.

2. **Hire Skilled Professionals**:
   - Assemble a team with diverse skills, as one person may not cover all aspects of pen testing.
   - Consider having a mix of experts in network, application, and database pen testing.

3. **Balance Cost vs. Benefits**:
   - Choose tests that provide the most benefit for the cost.
   - Example: A high-cost test may be less cost-effective compared to a cheaper test that provides most of the required results.

4. **Use Methodologies**:
   - Follow established methodologies, like the EC-Council LBT Methodology, to ensure a thorough and systematic approach.

5. **Document Results Carefully**:
   - Create a comprehensible report for the client, avoiding excessive technical jargon but maintaining accuracy.

6. **Internal Pen Testing Teams**:
   - Internal pen testers should follow the same guidelines as hired external teams.

7. **Timing of Pen Tests**:
   - Perform pen tests after infrastructure changes, discovery of new threats, hardware/software updates, or significant policy changes.

8. **Ethics in Pen Testing**:
   - Obtain explicit written permission from clients.
   - Adhere to nondisclosure agreements.
   - Test tools in isolated environments first and document results.
   - Inform clients of potential risks and notify them immediately of highly valuable flaws.
   - Deliver social engineering test results in a generalized format, avoiding specific employee details.
   - Maintain a clear ethical distinction between a security professional and a criminal hacker.

9. **Evolving as a Pen Tester**:
   - Stay current with technological changes and advancements.
   - Seek knowledge outside your job through courses, labs, conferences, and workshops.
   - Join security groups and stay updated on current topics.
   - Continuously update your skill set and knowledge base.
   - Read relevant books, journals, and trade magazines, and visit security websites and forums.

10. **Learning Resources**:
   - **YouTube**: Check channels like Hack Nine and EC-Council for recent content and courses.
   - **Libraries and Bookstores**: Visit places like Barnes & Noble to find books on hacking, protocols, networking, Dev, IoT, and other related technologies.

11. **Qualifications and Experience**:
   - **Experience Conundrum**: Entry-level jobs often require experience, which creates a paradox for newcomers. Building experience through roles in system administration or consultancy can help transition into pen testing.
   - **Career Path**: If starting from a non-IT job, build foundational tech skills and seek roles in IT-related areas to gain relevant experience.

12. **Essential Skills**:
   - **Networking**: Understand host-to-host communications, networking components (routers, switches), and protocols (TCP/IP, OSI model). Be able to assemble and troubleshoot networks.
   - **Ethical Hacking**: Learn hacking techniques, tools, and methodologies through courses like CEH and OSCP.
   - **Open Source Technologies**: Familiarize yourself with open source tools like MySQL and Apache, and various Linux distributions.
   - **Wireless and Protocols**: Know about wireless technologies (802.11x, 802.1x) and other protocols like Bluetooth.
   - **Troubleshooting**: Develop strong troubleshooting skills to resolve issues efficiently. Creativity and expertise in various areas enhance problem-solving abilities.
   - **Databases**: Gain knowledge of relational database management systems (RDBMS) like Oracle, SQL Server, MySQL, and DB2.
   - **Operating Systems**: Be proficient in Windows, Linux, and Mac. Mainframe experience is less common but useful.
   - **Web Application and Server Knowledge**: Understand web application architecture, HTTP methods, and server operations.

13. **Communication Skills**:
   - **Interpersonal Skills**: Be able to communicate effectively with clients and team members.
   - **Technical Explanations**: Explain technical findings clearly to non-technical clients.
   - **Report Writing**: Produce detailed and comprehensive pen test reports, often lengthy and complex. Presentation skills are also crucial for summarizing findings to clients.

14. **Building a Profile**:
   - **Research and Publications**: Conduct research, publish papers, and present at conferences.
   - **Certifications**: Obtain relevant certifications (e.g., CEH, OSCP) and be a member of reputable organizations.
   - **Book Writing**: Consider writing books or chapters on security topics.
   - **Experience**: Continuously work on and document your experience and achievements in security and pen testing.

15. **Pen Tester Responsibilities**:
   - **Risk Assessment**: Assess and prioritize risks in target systems.
   - **Goal Definition**: Clearly define the goals of your pen test.
   - **Result Communication**: Communicate findings effectively, including vulnerability justifications and risk mitigation proposals.
   - **Understanding Systems**: Be knowledgeable about the organization’s infrastructure, including servers, networks, and security protocols.
   - **Business Understanding**: Have a basic understanding of business operations to address risks appropriately.
