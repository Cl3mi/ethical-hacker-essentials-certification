- **Cyber Kill Chain Overview:**
    
    - **Stages:** Reconnaissance, weaponization, delivery, exploitation, installation, command and control, action on objectives.
    - **Concept:** Each stage is a link in a chain; breaking any link stops the attack.

- **Analogy:**    
    - Like a bike chain, if any link breaks, the attack cannot proceed.

- **Example Scenario:**
    - **Reconnaissance:** Attacker identifies five target employees.
    - **Weaponization:** Creates a malicious email attachment.
    - **Delivery:** Sends the email to the targets.

- **Employee Responses:**
    - **Employees 1 & 2:** Delete the suspicious email.
    - **Employee 3:** Quarantines the email.
    - **Employee 4:** Takes similar action.
    - **Employee 5:** Clicks the link, triggering the malware.

- **Malware Progression:**
    - **Exploitation:** Malware runs on employee's computer.
    - **Antivirus Action:** Detects and stops the malware during installation.

- **Points of Intervention:**
    - **Pre-Exploitation:** Blocking malicious emails could have prevented the delivery.
    - **Post-Exploitation:** Antivirus stopped the installation.
    - **Other Stages:** Harder to intervene at weaponization but possible during command and control or action on objectives.

- **Key Takeaway:**
    - Early intervention in the kill chain can prevent attacks; robust endpoint protection is crucial for stopping malware.
